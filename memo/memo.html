Executive memo · Procure-to-pay

Paying suppliers takes 79 days; rework and blocks cost effort more than time

ToHead of Procurement Operations FromKhandaker Abrar Yasir, process analyst Date13 November 2026 BasisPurchase-order event log (BPI Challenge 2019): 251,734 PO line items, 1.6 million events. Time figures use POs created January to July 2018, because clearing data stops in January 2019.
  1. The process is slow mainly because of when invoices are paid, not how they are processed. An item takes a median 79.3 days from order to payment (one in ten takes over 129 days). 58% of that time comes after the invoice is booked; 91% of items are paid more than two weeks after both goods and invoice are in.
  2. The three-way-match control leaks. 9.1% of items (11,815 of 129,244) had the invoice booked before the goods were received. One supplier accounts for 18% of these; ten suppliers for 46%.
  3. Rework is common but buys little time when removed. 36% of paid items had a price or quantity change, a cancellation or a payment block, and they took about 18 days longer. A simulation shows that halving payment blocks saves only 1.3 days at the median, yet raises the share of untouched items from 64% to 75%.

Packaging, the largest spend area (60,000 items), stands out: median 100 days to payment against 56 for Sales, and 31% of items blocked. The model of the standard flow fits 92% of completed items; most of the rest have repeated goods receipts or invoices (partial deliveries).

Recommended changes

A to-be process was drawn and three changes tested in a simulation built from the observed items (500,000 simulated items per scenario; 95% intervals in brackets). The simulated as-is process reproduces the real 90th percentile within 0.1 day and the median within 1.5 days.

What this does not promise

  • Large time savings. Payment timing sets the cycle time. Cutting it means changing payment terms or payment runs, a finance decision outside this review.
  • Proven effects. The simulation assumes an item that avoids rework behaves like today's items without it. Harder orders may attract both rework and delay, so the savings are an upper bound for a given reduction.
  • The 50% reductions. These are assumptions. A pilot must measure how many blocks and changes the new checks prevent.
  • Workload. Fewer blocks and changes free Accounts Payable and Purchasing effort, but the log has no staffing data, so this is not quantified.

Data caveats

  • Clearing events stop in January 2019, so time figures use POs from January to July 2018.
  • Vendor-invoice dates carry no time of day; 100 events with impossible dates are excluded.
  • Setting a payment block is rarely logged; removals stand in for blocks.
  • Data is anonymised; targets are proposals, not company policy.

Decisions requested

  1. Approve a three-month pilot of tolerance-based matching with the ten suppliers that hold 37% of payment blocks, measuring block rate (K8) and untouched rate (K6) monthly on the dashboard.
  2. Ask IT to enforce goods receipt before invoice booking for this flow, starting with the supplier behind 18% of early bookings.
  3. Agree the KPI targets in the catalogue (e.g. median 73 days, early bookings ≤ 5.5%, blocks ≤ 25%) as the baseline for the pilot.
  4. Share payment-term data with the analysis team, so the 46-day wait after invoice booking can be explained and, if useful, discussed with Finance.
